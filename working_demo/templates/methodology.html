Methodology — BSI v3 + 5-Gate Trade Architecture

BSI v3 = consumer alt-data composite (8 pillars, MOVE excluded as macro context). 5-gate architecture = pre-registered trade-firing rule with 4 mascot archetypes. SECTION 00

The logic in plain English

Before the equations and the gates, this is what the methodology actually does, in one paragraph and a diagram. Read this first if you have never seen the project before; skip it if you have.
THE WHOLE THING IN ONE BREATH
Consumers struggle online before companies admit it on their books. We measure the online struggling. The companies show up on the books later. We trade between the two.
Everything else on this page is implementation detail.
STEP 1 · Pick the right firms
27 non-bank consumer lenders — BNPL, subprime auto, marketplace lenders, card monolines. Companies that lend small amounts to people who barely qualify.
STEP 2 · Watch the customers, not the firms
Companies report quarterly and lie. Customers complain in real time and don't.
STEP 3 · Listen in eight places at once
CFPB complaints, Reddit, Bluesky, app-store reviews, Google searches, ABS metrics, 8-K language — plus MOVE for macro context.
STEP 4 · Squash the eight signals into one number
Weight them, exponentially-weighted-moving-average (EWMA) z-score each, gate the missing ones to zero, sum it up. That is BSI. One number per firm per day.
STEP 5 · When BSI spikes, the cascade has started
T+0 BSI fires. T+1 charge-offs and DPD move. T+2 junior ABS spreads widen. T+3 equity drops. T+4 defaults crystallize.
STEP 6 · The trade lives at T+2 / T+3, never at T+4
By the time the company actually defaults, everything has already happened. Get in at T+0; exit by T+3.
STEP 7 · The loss math underneath is the standard one
Loss = PD × LGD × EAD. BSI predicts the change in PD. LGD ≈ 100% on first-loss tranches. EAD = trade size. So the question reduces to: does BSI lead changes in PD?

SECTION 01

BSI v3 — composite specification

The Behavioural Stress Index (BSI) is an exponentially-weighted moving average (EWMA) z-score composite of consumer-credit-distress signals. The v3 specification (current) excludes MOVE (macro market data, not alternative data) and assigns weight to 8 alt-data pillars.
WHAT BSI ACTUALLY MEASURES · THE VOLUME LOGIC
BSI tracks spikes in volume of consumer-distress signals — relative to each firm's own recent history, fused across eight parallel public-data feeds.
Seven of eight pillars are volume-derived (CFPB complaint count, app-store review count, Reddit post count, Bluesky post count, Google Trends search-interest, ABS DPD-bucket count, 8-K filing count). Each is normalised against the firm's own EWMA baseline so a 100-→-250 spike on a small firm carries the same z-score as a 1,000-→-2,500 spike on a large firm. The eighth pillar (FRED macro composite + MOVE rates-vol gate) is market-data context, not a volume series.
Three smart refinements on top of raw volume: (i) compositional filtering (CFPB distress-share vs total), (ii) cross-pillar concordance (8 simultaneous spikes are harder to dismiss than one), (iii) per-firm EWMA normalisation (cross-firm comparable z-scores).
WHAT IS EWMA?
EWMA = exponentially-weighted moving average. A running mean (and running standard deviation) where today's observation gets the most weight, yesterday's slightly less, the day before slightly less than that, decaying exponentially backward. An observation from one year ago carries roughly half the weight of today's observation in our specification (half-life H = 250 trading days).
We compare today's value to that running mean and divide by the running standard deviation. If today's reading is two running-standard-deviations above the running mean, we say z = 2 — that's a meaningful spike against the firm's own history.
Why EWMA instead of a simple moving average? Because EWMA is responsive to fresh changes (the recent days dominate) while still anchored to the firm's longer-run normal. A simple moving average would either be too sticky (long window) or too jumpy (short window). EWMA splits the difference smoothly.

SECTION 01.5

Publication-lag treatment & look-ahead-bias defence

The most common silent failure in alt-data backtests is using information that wasn't actually visible at the time the signal claims to fire. This section documents how BSI v3 prevents that and what to verify in the pod's live deployment.
THE RISK · LOOK-AHEAD BIAS
CFPB complaints carry two dates: received_date (when the consumer filed it) and publication_date (when CFPB scrubbed for PII and made it public). The publication lag is typically 30–90 days, sometimes longer for narrative complaints.
A naive backtest that uses "all complaints with received_date ≤ T" at every simulated date T is silently using complaints that did not exist in the public database on date T — they were only published weeks or months later. That is look-ahead bias. The signal would secretly know the future.
THE FIX · PHASE 2Q PUBLICATION-LAG CALIBRATION
Phase 2Q empirically calibrated the CFPB publication lag — measured how long, on average, complaints take to appear in the public DB after they were filed. Median lag ≈ 45 days; tail to 90+.
RULE. BSIt uses only complaints with publication_date ≤ t, not received_date ≤ t.
That single rule transforms the backtest from a look-ahead-biased simulation into an honest one. It is the antidote to cheating, not the cheating.
BACKTEST vs. LIVE DEPLOYMENT — DIFFERENT MECHANICS, SAME PRINCIPLE
Mode Risk Mitigation
Backtest (historical) Could use future-published complaints to compute past signals Filter by publication_date ≤ T at every simulated date T (Phase 2Q)
Live deployment Could retroactively rebuild old BSI rows when new late-published complaints arrive Point-in-time freeze: store BSIt with issued_at timestamp; never overwrite
POINT-IN-TIME SCHEMA · WAREHOUSE TABLE bsi_daily
observed_at DATE — the date the BSI value is FOR
issued_at TIMESTAMP — the date / time the BSI value was COMPUTED
weights_hash VARCHAR — pre-registered weight version used
freeze_flag BOOLEAN — TRUE = locked, FALSE = recomputable
If issued_at ≈ observed_at + publication-lag and freeze_flag = TRUE, the row is point-in-time honest. If issued_at is much more recent than observed_at, the row was retroactively recomputed and may carry information that did not exist at observed_at.
ONE-LINE DEFENCE
The publication-lag calibration is the methodology that prevents look-ahead bias. Without it, BSI would silently use information that did not exist at the time it claims to fire. With it, every backtest fire is reproducible from data that a real-time observer could have seen on that date.

SECTION 03.5

Bifurcated long-vs-short routing — the central thesis

The 5-gate trade-firing rule is direction-agnostic: it tells you the BSI signal is real and the firm is in stress. Direction (SHORT vs LONG) is determined by a second-stage filter that combines denominator normalisation, survival vs collapse trajectory, and archetype voting on the appropriate side. This is the central thesis of the framework.
Trade fires & SHORT ⇔ G1..G5 satisfied ∧ denom-normalised z still elevated ∧ short-pod votes ≥ threshold
Trade fires & LONG ⇔ G1..G5 satisfied (lagged) ∧ denom-normalised z RECOVERED ∧ long-pod 6-condition recovery test passes
Filter stageRoutes to SHORTRoutes to LONG
Stage 1 — BSI signalBSI fires (z≥2.0). Same trigger for both routes.
Stage 2 — Denominator normalisation Raw and normalised z both elevated → distress is structural Raw z elevated, normalised z calm → growth-stage false positive (e.g., SEZL)
Stage 3 — 5-gate confirmation G1 ∧ G2 SCP ∧ G3 MOVE ∧ G4 CCD ∧ G5 FDS at stress trigger G1 lagged + 6-condition recovery test (Fib + U/D ratio + decay)
Stage 4 — Archetype voting SHORT pod: Renaissance, Bridgewater, Two Sigma, Millennium, Citadel, BearWatch LONG pod: Buffett, Pabrai, Marks, Klarman, Greenblatt, Druckenmiller
Stage 5 — Position routing BLITZ / SCOUT / GUARDIAN size by gate-count strictness Size by Druckenmiller asymmetric-risk filter (R:R ≥ 3:1, tight stop)
▶ EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE FOR BIFURCATION (paper §6.9 + §10.13)
Naive equity wrapper: "short every BSI fire" produces calendar-time α with t = 0.08 (statistical zero) on CAPM / Fama-French 3-factor regressions, 2018-2024. The WIN cases (CVNA +96%, UPST +88%) are averaged with the LOSS case (SEZL −600% if shorted), netting to zero. This is the empirical motivation for the entire framework.
Bifurcated wrapper: the same BSI signal, routed by denominator normalisation + 5-gate + archetype voting, produces +30.85% mean projected TRS return on the Phase 2B stylised model (4 firms with credit-instrument coverage; stylised, not realised) AND correctly avoids the SEZL false positive in Phase 2C archetype backtest (SCOUT and GUARDIAN both BLOCK on SEZL because G5 doesn't fire). The framework is the answer to the null.
Why this is publishable: the t = 0.08 calendar-time α is no longer a "honest null we can't explain" — it's diagnostically attributed to instrument-selection (paper §7.2), with a structural response (the bifurcated architecture). Pretending the naive strategy works would have gotten the paper rejected; reporting the null AND the framework's response makes the paper substantially stronger.
SECTION 04

🤖 ROBO meta-archetype — population layer + cold-start protocol

ROBO is the algorithmic ensemble layer that bootstraps from a synthetic-user prior at launch and progressively transitions to live user population as the user base grows. Monte Carlo (Phase 2D) calibrates the transition.
ROBO_population(t) = Σu reputation(u) · mascot_signal(u, t) · conviction(u, t)
ROBO_combined(t) = (1 − w_real) · ROBO_synthetic + w_real · ROBO_population

N < 50100%0%SYNTHETIC ONLY (Monte Carlo prior)
50 ≤ N < 500linear blendlinear blendTRANSITIONING (w_real = (N − 50) / (500 − 50))

K_target = 500 derived from Phase 2D Monte Carlo: at K=500 synthetic agents under a four-tier heterogeneity prior (skilled 15%, average 50%, noisy 25%, adversarial 10%), the population layer reaches 96% accuracy under PnL-weighted reputation — within 1pp of the asymptote. Adversarial robustness: PnL-weighted reputation maintains 96% accuracy even with 30% adversarial agents (uniform-vote collapses to 20%). See /empirical §05.
